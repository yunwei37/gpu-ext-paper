\paragraph{Methodology}
Five application studies and one operator study use RTX 5090, Linux 6.15.11, NVIDIA 575.57.08,
400\,W, exclusive GPU execution and randomized complete blocks with
numerical/engagement checks. Matched native/BPF pairs share workload and
executor/actuator. Central values are medians across cells (POD uses means); ratios/percentage changes use paired
geometric means, except XSched's paired absolute/relative means and
Hummingbird's mean SLO percentage-point differences. All intervals are
whole-block 95\% bootstrap intervals. These adapter-based comparisons establish
neither formal equivalence nor original-hardware/headline reproduction.

\paragraph{Driver-path mechanism cost}
Separately, on RTX 5090/610.43.02, native/BPF share the UVM module and no-prefetch decision.
Fifteen paired blocks read one word per 64\,KiB of an 8\,GiB CPU-initialized
allocation; all 3,932,160 outputs match. Untimed tracing confirms zero prefetch.
The kernel-verified callback/checked setter increases CUDA-event kernel time
3.219\% [2.247\%, 4.202\%]: CPU-resident, non-first-touch fault cost, not
application speedup or payload bandwidth.

\paragraph{MoE-Infinity}
MoE-Infinity v3's activation-matrix matching, prefetch ranking and eviction~\cite{xue2025moeinfinity}
use a common GPT-OSS-120B frontend. Native/host-uBPF share prediction-set protection
and repaired execution. Prediction-off retains the upstream prefill extra-expert
overload shortcut; policy arms enforce strict pool budgets. Thus baseline
differences combine policy and executor effects. Five blocks yield
11.8964/11.2233/11.1900 token/s for baseline/native/BPF, including final prefetch
drain. BPF/native is 0.996540 [0.989239, 1.005508]. Against baseline, BPF loses
6.92\% throughput while reducing first-visible-text latency 22.48\%; this is
neither kernel-UVM policy execution nor isolated JIT cost.

\paragraph{XSched and GPreempt}
\Cref{fig:matched-scheduling} separates foreground latency from background
progress. The XSched~\cite{shen2025xsched} comparison uses a host-JIT HPF port
with original Level-1 suspend/resume. Ten four-arm blocks measure submission-to-first-CTA
p99 and completed background kernels/s. BPF-minus-XSched paired-mean p99 change is
$+0.292$\,s $[-0.109,+0.642]$. The different driver-timeslice policy lowers
queueing delay but loses 39.5\% of XSched's background throughput (paired-mean relative
change); it is not the matched HPF implementation.

GPreempt~\cite{fan2025gpreempt} measures arrival-to-synchronized-verified-response
p99 and in-window BE goodput over 60 seconds: VGG19 at 100 requests/s versus
ResNet152 at 100/200/continuous supply, five blocks each. Seeded FP32 TVM testing
models use batch-one CUDA graphs and 200\,$\mu$s preprocessing. Native uses one
prioritized-stream context; all arms use the same custom driver. C/BPF share two contexts and mapped-host flags
because original GDRCopy failed on this GPU. BPF combines host-JIT reset/hint/block/release
with kernel-BPF timeslices. Under continuous supply, C/native and BPF/native
p99 decrease 9.61\% [6.46\%, 11.91\%] and 10.81\% [9.91\%, 11.70\%], costing
about 9\% BE goodput; BPF/C changes $-1.32$\% $[-4.19\%,+0.51\%]$.
All 270,000 foreground requests complete in-window. Neither scheduling metric
measures a preemption quantum or permits cross-workload ranking.

\paragraph{Expert Buffering}
We port Section VI's inactive-first/LIFO expert residency policy~\cite{huang2023towards}
to the same Qwen/FineMoE whole-expert executor as a fixed-$K{=}16$ FIFO baseline.
Five randomized blocks yield 5.508/5.663/5.621 token/s for FIFO/native/host-uBPF.
Native/FIFO is $+2.55\%$ [$+2.10\%$,$+3.09\%$]; BPF/FIFO is
$+1.79\%$ [$+0.79\%$,$+2.81\%$]. Native and BPF make identical residency
decisions and reduce logical copy payload by 12.85\%, but BPF/native is
$-0.74\%$ [$-1.40\%$,$-0.20\%$], exposing a small mechanism-path cost.
This is a matched single-GPU policy port, not the original distributed system,
model, or reported throughput reproduction; logical bytes are not PCIe traffic.

\paragraph{FineMoE}
Five blocks compare demand-only, an all-positive ablation, native dynamic sets
and host-BPF~\cite{yu2026finemoe} with a shared Qwen1.5-MoE-A2.7B-Chat offloader.
Eight held-out MT-Bench prompts replace unavailable gated LMSYS data
($\leq$16 input, exactly 16 output tokens); 87,515,136 preflight FP32 values
and all 2,560 formal tokens match the reference. Throughput excludes final drain. BPF reduces completed prefetch
payload evicted unused by 60.41\% and raises throughput 24.64\% versus
all-positive, but loses 12.62\% throughput versus demand-only. BPF/native is
1.002107 [0.998266, 1.005226]. Payload counts are logical bytes, not PCIe traffic;
resident-unused copies remain unclassified. Selection, not BPF, explains the
all-positive comparison.

\paragraph{Hummingbird}
Using GPreempt's DNN frontend without CUDA graphs and 60-second response/goodput metrics,
all 50 cells cover periodic/BurstGPT arrivals and five blocks of native,
fixed-GPreempt, equal-timeslice, C and BPF arms.
C and host-uBPF-JIT implement the same paper-described splitting/idle-admission
component~\cite{hu2026hummingbird}, not the authors' full system.
BPF/fixed-GPreempt BE ratios are 0.80685 [0.79792, 0.81333] and
0.79693 [0.77145, 0.81358], respectively; C also loses throughput.
BPF/C BE ratios are 0.99428 [0.98598, 0.99960] and
0.99417 [0.95256, 1.03643].
Periodic BPF SLO attainment is 1.743 percentage points lower than C
[0.713, 3.483], using all offered requests and the frozen 1.811879\,ms threshold.
Neither port meets joint recovery/protection criteria; equivalence is not
established. Both share a conservative single-LP-launch completion fence,
unlike the paper's predicted pipeline; its causal cost was not isolated.
The source-transformed port uses only qualified output-copy gaps, without
prediction, multi-GPU offloading or multi-LP fairness evaluation.
Idle/fixed changes scheduling machinery, not only BPF execution. BurstGPT's
equal-timeslice control misses 317/30,000 in-window completions: its conditional
p99 cannot support a headline protection gain.

\paragraph{POD-Attention}
An actual device-BPF selector preserves POD's proportional SM-local task
choice~\cite{kamath2025pod}, including atomic claims and exhaustion fallback.
Five blocks compare serial/two-stream FlashAttention, inline POD, a CUDA-context
adapter and BPF across ten Llama-3-8B/Yi-6B attention shapes
(8192-token prefill, decode batches 32--192). All 250 full-output FP16
comparisons pass; maximum difference from original FlashAttention is
$6.104\!\times\!10^{-5}$. Separately, the original FP16 reference exceeds
the FP32 characterization tolerance in 150 prefill elements across repeated
scans; this is not full FP32 agreement. All 125,280 diagnostic BPF CTA choices
have exactly-once logical coverage. BPF increases mean CUDA-event latency
0.51--1.18\% versus the CUDA adapter in nine shapes and decreases it 0.44\%
in Llama/192; host-wall directions agree. It beats two-stream FlashAttention
in seven shapes, not all ten. A separate 15-cell (five-block)
Llama-3-8B/batch-32 phase decomposition isolates BPF/CUDA-adapter operator
cost at 1.78\% [1.64\%, 1.92\%] by CUDA event and 1.81\% [1.66\%, 1.96\%]
by synchronized host wall. Its median pre-Python interval is 271.225\,s versus
21.4\,ms for the adapter; this non-generic cold path bundles preload, agent,
PTX, and JIT work and is not attach latency. Fusion benefits
belong to POD; this explicit-ABI operator port is not its full serving system
or a strictly verified device policy.

\paragraph{Cross-layer raw state}
A five-block, 15-cell RTX 5090 campaign tests a per-GPU-thread ring against
independent CUDA truth and aggregate controls. Across the ten positive cells,
the host recovered exactly 34,560 distinct bounded raw tuples; the five
deliberate-overflow cells accounted for exactly 2,560 capacity drops and
rejected every incomplete stream. This is bounded device-to-host raw-record
expressibility and exact readback beyond reducible summaries, not a map
latency/bandwidth, on-chip-shard, automatic-placement, strict-verifier, or
arbitrary-data result.

\paragraph{Boundaries}
Existing-algorithm ports are not agent discoveries; agent generation alone
establishes no novelty. Historical single-round \Cref{fig:all-kernels-priority}
lacks scheduler-engagement evidence. Host-JIT parity proves neither
kernel admission nor enforced SIMT safety (\S\ref{sec:verification}).
