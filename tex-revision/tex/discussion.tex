\section{Discussion and Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Stale state and thrashing}
Delayed observations can select obsolete prefetches or retain cold pages even
when every individual request is legal. Transition validation protects the
covered driver mutation, not policy optimality or freedom from thrashing.
Policies should expire stale observations and bound speculative work;
hysteresis and bandwidth-aware admission require workload-specific evaluation.
The driver retains native memory-reclamation authority. Associative counters
can be merged across map shards, whereas eviction-list order or unique
ownership cannot be recovered by summing snapshots; such state needs a single
authority and explicit synchronization.

\paragraph{Tenants and software co-location}
The system-wide policy can distinguish registered processes, but does not
provide independently isolated policies for mutually distrustful tenants.
Per-tenant deployment would require attachment authorization, map namespacing,
per-hook budgets and driver arbitration of conflicting requests.
Our experiments use software co-location on an unpartitioned GPU, not MIG.
SemiAnalysis's criticism of fixed subdevices for large-model
inference~\cite{semianalysis2025partitioning} concerns that static partitioning
choice; it neither evaluates our software policies nor establishes their
production benefit. Co-location remains workload-dependent, with foreground
latency and background progress reported together.

\paragraph{Storage, CXL and portability}
A tiered-memory extension needs destination, lifetime, completion and failure
semantics in addition to placement policy. The asynchronous model can describe
such operations, but the current driver interface does not implement CXL or
disk transport. LMCache's local-disk backend is a separate framework-controlled
path, not evidence of transparent driver-managed storage paging. Mapping host
hooks to HMM/\texttt{migrate\_vma} or DRM is a portability direction, not a measured
AMD/Intel port. Device instrumentation additionally depends on available code,
helper semantics, compilation and launch/resource limits; PTX generation alone
does not support another vendor. Architected validated transition hooks could
reduce future driver coupling, but would not remove those implementation and
verification obligations.

\paragraph{Trampoline scaling}
The retained RTX~5090 runtime executes per-thread calls, so callback cost grows
with active work. At 4,096 blocks, its no-op increment is
0.0012--0.0022\,ms as active warps grow from 2,048 to 32,768; a counter
increment grows from 0.0204 to 0.5417\,ms. In a fixed-work follow-up, the
endpoint difference-in-differences estimate is $-2.70\%$ with a 95\% interval
of $[-5.47\%,3.13\%]$. Warp-level aggregation
is future work for this runtime.

\paragraph{Related mechanisms}

\texttt{sched\_ext}~\cite{schedext-docs}, \texttt{cache\_ext}~\cite{zussman2025cache_ext}, and XDP provide programmable Linux hooks; \sys targets cross-device resource effects (\S\ref{sec:design-interfaces}). Driver-level GPU resource managers~\cite{kato2011timegraph,kato2012gdev,kernelintercept,fan2025gpreempt,wang2024gcaps,coppock2025lithos} put policy and privileged mechanisms in the driver trust boundary. Their update and recovery costs depend on the implementation, rather than every driver modification being inherently unsafe. User-space frameworks~\cite{ng2023paella,shen2025xsched,gim2025pie,chen2025ktransformers} can coordinate registered applications, but their controls differ from fault-time and cross-context driver hooks (\S\ref{sec:policy-mechanism}).
NVBit~\cite{villa2019nvbit} and Neutrino~\cite{neutrino} provide programmable GPU instrumentation, which alone does not establish safe policy enforcement. eGPU~\cite{egpu} extends eBPF to GPU instrumentation with scalar per-thread execution.
